I am a PhD candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Oklahoma, specializing in power systems and energy technologies. My research focuses on the aging of electrical machines operating under dynamic operating conditions imposed by grid flexibility provision impact the lifetime of electric motors. I am interested in exploring control and data-driven techniques to solve challenges related to grid modernization.

Data Driven Temperature Estimation Techniques Using Supervised Machine Learning
  • Investigated the feasibility of linear regression models for real-time temperature estimation in a Permanent Magnet Synchronous Machine (PMSM) to prevent overheating and failure.
  • Implemented and evaluated a set of regression models (Ordinary Least Squares, Ridge, and Lasso) in Python (Scikit-learn library) by standardizing and preprocessing a large-scale dataset (300 MB) of motor operational parameters.
  • Demonstrated that linear models were insufficient (achieving a max R-squared value of 0.29 for the multivariable model), proving that the motor's thermal behavior is dominated by complex non-linear relationships.
  • Highlighted the need for advanced regression models like autoregression to capture the sytem memory to increase accuracy.
  • Technologies/Skills: Python, Scikit-learn, Supervised Machine Learning, Linear Regression, Data Preprocessing
Optimal Placement and Sizing of Battery Energy Storage System
  • Implemented a two-step approach using the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm in Python (pandapower library) for the optimal placement and size of a Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) in an electrical network.
  • Demonstrated that optimum BESS placement on IEEE 14-bus and 30-bus test systems significantly reduced the power losses by 7.5%, proving that placing the BESS at the highest-load bus provides the most significant grid efficiency gains from local energy support.
  • Technologies/Skills: Python, pandapower, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Power Flow Analysis
Analyzing and Forecasting Electricity Demand
  • Analyzed ERCOT electricity demand data (2016-2021) by conducting time series decomposition to identify trends, seasonality and correlations with external factors (temperature, GDP)
  • Developed a monthly forecasting model (seasonal ARIMA(0,1,2)(0,1,1)[12]), achieving a high-accuracy MAPE of <5% to support long term operational and resource planning.
  • Developed a different daily forecasting model using dynamic regression to capture the complex non-linear effects of temperature and weekday/weekend patterns on short-term demand.
  • Presented insights recommending analyzing larger dataset to train advanced models such as neural networks and fuzzy logic.
  • Skills: R Programming, Time Series Analysis, ARIMA
